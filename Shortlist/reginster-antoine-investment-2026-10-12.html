How we’re screening these

Cap Rate = Annual Net Income ÷ Purchase Price Annual net income = (monthly rent × 12) − annual property taxes − annual HOA. Monthly figures on the cards are for readability; the math uses yearly totals.

Rent is a conservative estimate built from comparable units in the same building and of similar size. We weight current asking rents most heavily because they show today’s market, and use leases closed in the last six months as a cross-check, never going more than 8% above what has actually closed. It is not the previous tenant’s rent and not a guarantee. Taxes are estimated on the reassessment after your purchase (about 1.75% of the price), not the seller’s current bill, which is usually lower. HOA is taken directly from each listing.

This is a fast screen, not full underwriting. Insurance, property management, and a vacancy and repair allowance come once you’re serious about one unit, and would typically take roughly half a point to a full point off each figure. It is unlevered, which matches an all-cash purchase.

The negotiated figures show what the cap rate becomes if we buy 5% or 8% below asking, because the price you pay, not the asking price, sets your return. We favored units with long time on market, price reductions and low prior purchase prices. Each card lists the rental comps behind its rent estimate, each with its own link.
